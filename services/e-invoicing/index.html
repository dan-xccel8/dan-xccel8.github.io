E-Invoicing in the UAE

E-invoicing is mandatory in the UAE: businesses invoicing other businesses or government must exchange invoices electronically through an accredited provider, by a deadline set by revenue. The system

What e-invoicing actually is

The UAE Electronic Invoicing System replaces the invoice you email today with a structured electronic document that moves between businesses through accredited providers — with the FTA seeing the tax data as it flows. It runs on Peppol, using what is called the DCTCE five-corner model:

01

Your system

You raise the invoice in your accounting software or ERP, as you do today.

02

Your ASP

Your accredited service provider converts it to PINT-AE XML and transmits it.

03

The buyer's ASP

The buyer's own accredited provider receives and validates it on their side.

04

Your buyer

The invoice lands in the buyer's system, ready to book and pay — no re-keying.

05

The FTA

The fifth corner: tax data flows to the Federal Tax Authority in near real time.

  • An e-invoice is PINT-AE XML — a structured data file in the UAE's Peppol format. A PDF is not an e-invoice, even if your software generated it.
  • Exchange happens only through MoF-accredited ASPs. There is no direct or DIY route — your ASP transmits for you and registers your Peppol Participant ID.
  • Scope is B2B and B2G — invoices to businesses and government, regardless of whether you are VAT-registered. B2C is excluded for now.
  • The 14-day rule: invoices and credit notes must be issued and transmitted within 14 days.
If you do nothing

The cost of missing your deadline

AED 5,000 per month

The penalty under Cabinet Decision 106 of 2025 for failing to appoint an ASP or implement the system — charged for every month you remain non-compliant.

Fines from AED 100 per invoice

Individual invoices issued outside the system attract their own fines, starting from AED 100 each — a real number at any invoicing volume.

Invoices that are not legally valid

An invoice that never went through the system is not a legally valid e-invoice — expect buyers to reject it and disputes you do not need.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

+
Is a PDF an e-invoice?

No. Under the UAE Electronic Invoicing System, an e-invoice is a structured electronic document in PINT-AE XML format, exchanged between supplier and buyer through MoF-accredited service providers. A PDF — even one produced by accounting software and emailed to your customer — is not an e-invoice and does not meet the requirement.

Who must comply with UAE e-invoicing?

Every business that issues invoices to other businesses (B2B) or to government entities (B2G). The mandate is phased by revenue: businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more must appoint an ASP by 30 October 2026 and go live on 1 January 2027; businesses below AED 50 million appoint by 31 March 2027 and go live on 1 July 2027; government entities appoint by 31 March 2027 and go live on 1 October 2027. Business-to-consumer (B2C) invoicing is excluded for now. A voluntary phase has been open since 1 July 2026.

Do I have to comply if I am not VAT-registered?

Yes. The e-invoicing mandate applies to B2B and B2G transactions regardless of VAT registration, so a business below the VAT threshold — or not registered for VAT at all — still has to issue and transmit e-invoices through an accredited service provider once its phase begins.

What is an ASP, Peppol and PINT-AE?

An ASP is an Accredited Service Provider — a provider accredited by the Ministry of Finance (under Ministerial Decision 64 of 2025) to exchange e-invoices on your behalf; only ASPs may transmit e-invoices, and your ASP registers your Peppol Participant ID. Peppol is the international network and rulebook the UAE system runs on, using the DCTCE five-corner model. PINT-AE is the UAE's Peppol invoice format — the structured XML your invoice must be issued in.

Can I keep my current ERP or accounting software?

Yes. Microsoft, SAP, Sage, Oracle, Salesforce and other popular systems can be integrated through our MoF-accredited ASP partner, so your team keeps invoicing from the system they know. If you are on — or move to — Zoho Books, e-invoicing is native, because Zoho is itself an accredited ASP. And if you want no integration at all, you can upload invoices to a dedicated portal and have them transmitted compliantly for you.

What happens if I miss my deadline?

Under Cabinet Decision 106 of 2025, failing to appoint an ASP or implement the system carries a penalty of AED 5,000 per month, and individual invoices attract fines starting from AED 100 each. Invoices issued outside the system are also not legally valid e-invoices. If your appoint-by date has already passed, appoint an ASP as soon as possible — every month of delay adds to the exposure.
