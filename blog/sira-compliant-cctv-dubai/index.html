CCTV in Dubai: getting SIRA compliance right the first time

X XCCEL8 Team
7 October 2026·4 min read

In Dubai, CCTV is not just a security choice — it is a regulated one. The Security Industry Regulatory Agency (SIRA) sets the rules for who can install surveillance systems and what those systems must do, and getting it wrong means failed inspections, rework and, in some cases, fines. Yet many businesses only discover the rules after the cameras are already on the wall.

Here is what Dubai businesses need to know before they buy a single camera.

Why SIRA exists — and why it matters to you

SIRA regulates the security industry in Dubai to make sure surveillance systems actually work when they are needed. That means approved equipment, certified installers and systems designed to real standards — not a box of cameras from an electronics shop mounted wherever they fit. For regulated sectors and many commercial premises, SIRA compliance is not optional; it is a condition of operating.

The practical consequence: your CCTV installer must be SIRA-certified, and the system design must meet SIRA’s technical requirements. An uncertified installation can fail inspection even if the cameras themselves are excellent.

The mistakes businesses make most

The most expensive mistake is installing first and asking about compliance later. We regularly see systems that need partial or full rework: cameras at the wrong heights, blind spots at entrances, recording retention that falls short of requirements, or equipment that was never on the approved list. Every one of these is cheaper to get right on paper than to fix on a ladder.

Other common gaps: no proper site survey before installation (camera positions guessed, not planned), storage sized for days instead of the required retention period, no remote monitoring setup for the owner, and zero documentation of the system for inspections or insurance. A compliant system is a designed system — surveyed, specified and documented before installation day.

What a compliant system actually includes

A proper SIRA-compliant CCTV installation in Dubai starts with a site survey: entry and exit points, cash-handling areas, parking, perimeters and internal zones, mapped against what the regulations require for your type of premises. Then the equipment is specified — cameras with the right resolution and low-light performance for each position, network video recorders sized for the retention period, and secure remote access so you can check in from anywhere.

Installation follows the design: structured cabling to each camera point, protected recording equipment, clean labelling and a full handover — system documentation, user training and warranty terms. That documentation is what an inspector asks for, and it is what most non-compliant systems are missing.

Choosing the right equipment

Not all cameras are equal, and the cheapest option usually costs more in the long run. Business-grade systems from established manufacturers — Hikvision and similar professional ranges — offer the resolution, night vision, analytics and reliability that compliance and real security both demand. Features like motion detection, line-crossing alerts and number-plate recognition turn cameras from passive recording into active protection.

Just as important is integration. Modern premises rarely need CCTV alone — access control, intruder alarms and intercoms should work as one system. Planning them together avoids the all-too-common sight of three separate systems from three vendors that never talk to each other. Our physical security practice designs CCTV, access control and alarms as a single integrated setup.

The inspection is not the finish line

Passing inspection is the start, not the end. Cameras drift out of alignment, lenses get dirty, hard drives fill up and fail, firmware goes unpatched. A system nobody maintains quietly degrades until the day you actually need the footage — and discover it is not there. An annual maintenance contract keeps the system inspected, cleaned, tested and documented, so compliance is continuous rather than a one-time event.

Getting it right the first time

SIRA-compliant CCTV is one of those things that is straightforward when planned and painful when improvised. The formula is simple: certified installer, proper survey, approved equipment, documented handover, ongoing maintenance. Skip any step and you are likely to pay for it twice.
