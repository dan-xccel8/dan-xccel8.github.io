Patched but still exposed: the NetScaler flaw your September update didn't fix

X XCCEL8 Team
15 October 2026·6 min read

In late September, IT teams across the region worked through the same drill: Citrix had disclosed NetScaler flaws under active attack, and everyone rushed to get the fixed builds installed. Boxes were ticked, patch windows were logged, and the feeling was one of relief. Done.

Not done. Early October brought another NetScaler security bulletin from Citrix — and it changed the picture. The September patches (for CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772) do not cover this one. If your appliance is configured the wrong way — for this flaw, “wrong” means the normal way — you are still exposed, and you need to upgrade again.

This is CVE-2026-88779, Citrix bulletin CTX697174: an unauthenticated memory overflow (CWE-119) in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway, rated 8.7 under CVSS v4.0. It has been confirmed as actively exploited in the wild, and on 4 October it was added to the US CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue. The US federal remediation deadline of 7 October is a signal of severity, not a compliance date for UAE businesses — but it tells you how seriously the industry is taking this.

Why the September build doesn’t cover it

The September flaws and this one share a manufacturer, not a fix. CVE-2026-88779 lives in the SAML handling: it affects NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway when configured as a SAML Service Provider or a SAML Identity Provider. The September patches addressed different code paths. So an appliance upgraded in September to, say, 14.1-73.37 is still vulnerable to the October flaw if the SAML condition applies.

This is the trap: “we already patched” feels safe, and for most advisories it would be. Not here. If your team has NetScaler on the books, the question to ask is not “did we patch in September?” — it is “did we install the October builds?”

Who is actually at risk

The good news is that the blast radius is narrow, and it is easy to check whether you are inside it. This flaw only affects customer-managed appliances — the NetScaler ADC or Gateway boxes your team (or your IT partner) administers — and only when SAML is configured, either as a Service Provider or as an Identity Provider.

Citrix-managed cloud services are out of scope: the Gateway Service and Citrix-managed Adaptive Authentication were patched by the vendor, so there is nothing for you to do on those. If everything you use sits in Citrix’s managed cloud, you can stop reading and go back to your day.

For customer-managed appliances, checking your configuration is straightforward. In the NetScaler CLI or GUI, look for SAML configuration objects — commands like add authentication samlAction or add authentication samlIdPProfile. If either exists in your running config, the precondition is met and you are in scope. This check takes minutes, and it is exactly where a proper netscaler vulnerability check uae engagement starts: not with panic, but with a precise answer to “are we affected at all?”

What the fix looks like — and what most teams skip

The fix is an upgrade to the fixed builds: 14.1-73.46, 13.1-64.29, 14.1-73.46 FIPS, or 13.1-37.283 (or later). That part is well documented. The part most teams skip is what happens after the patch.

Because this flaw is already being exploited in the wild, patching today does not erase what happened yesterday. The sensible post-patch routine is the verification nobody schedules:

  • Log review: look for signs of attempted or successful exploitation in the weeks before the patch went on — repeated malformed SAML requests, unexplained service interruptions.
  • Crash and reboot history: a memory-overflow flaw of this type shows its face as instability. Check the crash logs and reboot history of the appliance over the past few weeks. An unexplained outage in September may have been this, not a bad cable.
  • Integrity spot-checks: Citrix states there is no impact on the integrity of customer data. Verify anyway — trust, but check the authentication configuration for anything that looks altered.

One more word on scope: the confirmed impact is denial of service. Repeated triggering can keep the service down. Some researchers have raised the possibility of remote code execution, but that is unconfirmed — treat any vendor or forum claim of “confirmed RCE” with scepticism. Facts first, speculation later.

A fourth bulletin — this one lands on the fixed builds themselves

On 8 October, Citrix published another NetScaler security bulletin (CTX697191) for CVE-2026-107406: a memory-overflow flaw in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway, rated 9.5 Critical under CVSS v4.0. It lives in the same SAML code area as CVE-2026-88779 — and this time, whether you are exposed depends on the build you are running.

On builds 14.1-73.37 to 14.1-73.41 and 13.1-64.23 to 13.1-64.28 (the builds shipped in the October bulletin — including the “fixed builds” named above), appliances configured as a SAML Identity Provider are affected. On older builds, both SAML Service Provider and Identity Provider configurations are affected. Secure Private Access hybrid deployments that use NetScaler instances must upgrade those instances too.

Two distinctions worth keeping straight. Citrix describes this flaw as something that “may lead to remote code execution or denial of service” — that is Citrix’s own assessment for this CVE, not the unconfirmed-RCE situation with CVE-2026-88779. And unlike the earlier October flaws, Citrix reports no confirmed exploitation in the wild for this one so far. With a 9.5 rating on a SAML surface attackers are already watching, “not yet exploited” is not a reason to wait.

The fixed builds for CVE-2026-107406 — 14.1-73.46, 13.1-64.29, 14.1-73.46 FIPS, and 13.1-37.283 (or later) — also contain the fixes from the two earlier October bulletins, so a single upgrade covers all three. There is no workaround listed. Citrix-managed cloud services and Citrix-managed Adaptive Authentication are patched by the vendor; nothing to do there.

The question to ask your team is no longer “did we install the October builds?” — it is “are we on 14.1-73.46 / 13.1-64.29 or later?”

The pattern is the real story

Step back, and the individual CVEs matter less than the rhythm. This is the fourth NetScaler security bulletin Citrix has disclosed in roughly six weeks. Different flaws, different fixes, the same urgent drumbeat — each one requiring someone to notice the bulletin, check the configuration, plan the window, apply the build and verify it afterwards.

That is not a one-off task; it is a treadmill. And treadmills need someone standing on them. Businesses that run their own firewalls and gateways without a standing patching discipline keep learning about these disclosures the expensive way: from an outage, or from an auditor, or from this blog post — weeks after the fact.

This is the unglamorous half of cyber security: not the dramatic breach response, but the quiet routine of watching advisories, matching them against your actual estate, and acting before the attackers do.
