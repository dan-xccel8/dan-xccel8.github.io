Phishing emails look real now: email security for UAE businesses

X XCCEL8 Team
23 November 2026·8 min read

The old advice — look for the spelling mistakes — no longer protects anyone. Today’s phishing emails are polished: correct logos, correct tone, correct signatures, sent from addresses a glance cannot fault, and increasingly written to sound exactly like the colleague, bank or courier they imitate. They ask for the ordinary things that do extraordinary damage — a sign-in on a lookalike page, an invoice paid to updated bank details, a document opened at the wrong moment. Defending a business against them is no longer about teaching staff to spot a bad email; it is about building layers that assume a convincing email will reach a busy person, and making sure that moment does not become a breach. That layered approach is the core of our cyber security services.

This guide explains what those layers are, in the order a business should build them.

Key takeaways

  • Modern phishing is well-written, well-branded and well-timed — visual inspection by a busy employee is no longer a defence on its own.
  • The highest-value habit in any business is verification out of band: bank-detail changes and payment requests confirmed through a separate, known channel, every time.
  • Email security tooling now goes far beyond spam filtering — checking links and attachments safely, and flagging impersonation of your own executives and suppliers.
  • Authentication standards working behind the scenes stop criminals sending email that claims to be from your domain, protecting your customers as well as your staff.

Why can’t staff just spot phishing emails any more?

Because the tell-tale signs have been engineered away. Templates are copied from the real organisations, the language is fluent, and the targeting is informed — the email references your actual supplier, your real delivery, your managing director’s actual name and style. It arrives when the recipient is busiest, because that is when it works. Asking people to catch all of that by eye is asking them to win an unfair contest every single day. People remain a layer of the defence — a vital one, for reporting and for verification habits — but a sane strategy stops expecting them to be the wall.

What is the single most valuable habit a business can build?

Verify the money out of band. The costliest phishing outcome for most businesses is not a virus; it is a payment sent to a criminal — an invoice from a real supplier with changed bank details, an urgent instruction apparently from the owner. The countermeasure costs nothing: any change to payment details, and any unusual payment request, is confirmed through a separate channel using contact details you already hold — a phone call to the known number, never the one in the email. Written into the payment process, applied without exceptions and without embarrassment, that one habit removes the most profitable trick in the phishing book.

What does modern email security actually do?

More than filter spam, though it does that too. Current email security platforms inspect links at the moment they are clicked, open attachments in a safe sandbox before delivery, and detect impersonation by comparing sender behaviour and identity against the real patterns of your executives, suppliers and customers. They quarantine the message that would have been the incident, and they show you the attempts, so the vague sense that “we get a few of those” becomes evidence the business can act on. For a small IT setup, this layer quietly carries most of the defensive load — and it is designed to be managed, monitored and tuned, which is how we deploy it.

How does email authentication protect us?

It stops your own domain being the weapon. Authentication standards — SPF, DKIM and DMARC — tell receiving mail systems which servers may legitimately send email as your business, and what to do with mail that fails the check. Properly configured, criminals cannot send convincing phishing that genuinely comes from your domain to your customers, partners and staff. It also improves the deliverability of your real email. It is unglamorous, technical and entirely a set-once-properly job — and an astonishing number of businesses have it half-set, which is nearly the same as not set.

What should staff training look like now?

Short, specific and continuous rather than annual and theatrical. Teach the pattern, not the typos: urgency as a warning sign, requests that arrive outside the normal process, sign-in pages reached from links. Run occasional simulated phishing so staff practise in safety — tools like Sophos Phish Threat automate the simulations and the follow-up training — and, most importantly, build a culture where reporting a suspicious message, or admitting a click, is praised and never punished. The business that hears about a bad click within minutes has a fundamentally different incident from the one that hears about it on Friday. Training that produces fast reporting is security tooling in human form.

What happens when a click does get through?

Speed decides. The account’s password is changed and sessions closed, multi-factor authentication checked, the mailbox searched for the rules attackers quietly add to hide their replies, and the blast radius established before the all-clear. That response is dramatically easier when it is planned and the security layers above have logged what happened. Ask of your current setup: if a finance employee clicked a perfect fake at 11:00, what exactly would happen by 11:15? The quality of that answer is your real phishing posture.

Frequently Asked Questions

Our team is small — is phishing really aimed at businesses like ours?

Yes. Small businesses are targeted precisely because they have real money moving and thin defences, and automated campaigns make no distinction by size. The bank-detail change and fake-invoice attacks are aimed squarely at businesses like yours.

We have spam filtering. Isn’t that email security?

It is the first layer of it. Modern email security adds link and attachment inspection at click and delivery time, and impersonation detection aimed at your executives and suppliers — the categories spam filters alone were not built to catch. That is the difference between a basic filter and a dedicated platform such as Fortinet FortiMail or Barracuda Email Protection.

What is DMARC, in one sentence?

It is a published rule for your domain that tells the world’s mail systems how to treat email claiming to be from you that fails authentication — so criminals cannot convincingly send as your business.

How do we stop staff paying a fake invoice?

By process, not vigilance: payment-detail changes and unusual payment requests are verified through a separate known channel, every time, without exception. That one rule defeats the most profitable phishing attack on small businesses.

Should we run simulated phishing tests?

Yes, run kindly and used for teaching. They show where the real exposure sits and normalise reporting — which is the behaviour that limits damage when a real one lands.

Where should we start?

With the three quick wins: the verification rule for payments, a review of your email authentication, and an honest look at whether your email security goes beyond spam filtering. We can assess all three and build out the layers in that order.
