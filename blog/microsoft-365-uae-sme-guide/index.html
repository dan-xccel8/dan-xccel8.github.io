Microsoft 365 for UAE SMEs: what's worth paying for (and what's not)

X XCCEL8 Team
27 October 2026·4 min read

Almost every UAE office pays for Microsoft 365. Far fewer get their money’s worth. The usual pattern: every employee gets the same mid-tier licence, half the apps go unused, security settings stay at their defaults, and the company pays premium prices for a setup that behaves like basic email.

Microsoft 365 is genuinely good value — email, Office apps, cloud storage, Teams and serious security, all for a per-user monthly fee. But the value comes from matching licences to roles and configuring what you bought, not from buying one licence for everyone and hoping for the best.

Key takeaways

  • Not every employee needs the same licence — frontline and desk roles have very different needs, and mixing tiers (Basic, Standard, Premium) is how you stop overpaying.
  • The security features are the most underused part of the suite: multi-factor authentication and the built-in protections stop the attacks that actually hit SMEs.
  • SharePoint, OneDrive and Teams replace the shared-drive chaos — but only if files are deliberately moved there and permissions are set up properly.
  • An annual licence review pays for itself: leavers’ licences, unused apps and wrong tiers quietly add up.

The Licence Mix Mistake

Microsoft 365 comes in tiers, and the differences matter. The entry tier covers web and mobile Office apps with business email and cloud storage — enough for roles that live in email and a browser. The standard tier adds the full desktop Office apps. The premium tier adds advanced security: device management, stronger identity protection, and the tools to protect company data on personal devices.

Most companies buy one tier for everyone — usually the middle one — which means the receptionist gets desktop apps they never open while the finance laptop runs without the device protection it should have. Matching the tier to the role is the single biggest saving available, and it requires no technical work at all — just a list of who does what.

The Security You Already Paid For

Email is how most attacks arrive, and Microsoft 365’s built-in protections are specifically designed for that reality. Multi-factor authentication is the single highest-value setting in the entire suite: it stops the vast majority of account-takeover attempts, and enabling it across the company takes an afternoon.

The premium tier adds the layers that matter for growing businesses: conditional access policies that block logins from unexpected places, device management that lets you wipe company data from a lost laptop, and Defender protections that scan attachments and links before anyone clicks. Many companies pay for the premium tier and never switch these on — which is the IT equivalent of buying a safe and leaving it open.

The Apps Everyone Ignores

SharePoint and OneDrive together replace the shared network drive — with version history, so the “final_v7_ACTUAL.xlsx” problem disappears, and proper permissions instead of everyone seeing everything. Teams is more than video calls: it is where files, chats and meetings live together per project or department, replacing the scattered mix of WhatsApp groups and email threads.

Planner and To Do give small teams lightweight task management without buying another tool. Forms replaces paper surveys and paper-based data collection. None of these cost extra — they are in the licences already paid for, sitting unused in the app launcher.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Microsoft 365 include for a small business?

Business email (Exchange), the Office apps (Word, Excel, PowerPoint), cloud storage (OneDrive and SharePoint), Teams for meetings and collaboration, and built-in security features. Higher tiers add the desktop Office apps and advanced security and device-management tools.

Do all employees need the same licence?

No — and buying one tier for everyone is the most common way companies overpay. Roles that work in email and a browser are fine on the entry tier; only staff who need the full desktop Office apps or advanced security protections need the higher tiers.

What is multi-factor authentication and why does it matter?

Multi-factor authentication requires a second verification step — usually a phone approval — alongside the password. It blocks the large majority of account-takeover attempts, including the phishing attacks that target SMEs daily, and it is included in every Microsoft 365 tier.

Is our email secure with the default settings?

The defaults are reasonable but not complete. Multi-factor authentication should be enforced for everyone, and companies on the premium tier should have conditional access and device policies configured — these are the settings that stop real attacks.

How often should we review our licences?

At least once a year, and whenever staff join or leave. Leavers’ licences left active, new hires given the wrong tier, and apps nobody uses are how a tidy subscription quietly grows well beyond what the business needs.

Get Microsoft 365 working the way you pay for it — right-sized licences, security configured, apps actually adopted.
