Cabling done once, done right: structured cabling for UAE offices

X XCCEL8 Team
4 November 2026·6 min read

Structured cabling is the one part of your office network you cannot easily change later. Access points get upgraded, switches get replaced, phones get swapped — the cable inside the walls and ceilings stays, carrying all of it. That is why the cheapest cabling decision is the one you make once, properly, before the ceilings close: design it from the floor plan, install it to standard, label every point, and certify every link.

Done right, nobody ever thinks about the cabling again — it simply works, for every system that plugs into it, for years. Done cheaply, it becomes the hidden cause of slow networks, mystery dropouts and expensive fault-finding for the life of the office. This guide sets out what “done right” actually includes, and how to tell the difference before you sign off.

Key takeaways

  • Everything in a modern office runs on the cabling — Wi-Fi access points, IP cameras, phones, access control and the PCs themselves; wireless is only wireless for the last few metres.
  • “Done right” is a system, not a cable: floor-plan design, Cat6A copper and fibre where it counts, tidy racks and patch panels, and every point labelled at both ends.
  • Certification is the proof: Fluke testing for copper links and OTDR traces for fibre, with reports handed over per link — faults found are fixed and re-tested before sign-off.
  • Re-cabling an occupied office costs more and disrupts more than cabling a fit-out — which is why spare points and capacity belong in the first design, not a later retrofit.

Why does cabling decide how your office network performs?

Because every headline technology in the office depends on it. A Wi-Fi 6 or Wi-Fi 7 access point is only as fast as the cable feeding it — put a high-end access point on a poor or over-long run and you have paid for performance the building cannot deliver. The same applies to IP cameras drawing power over Ethernet, to desk phones, and to the switches linking your floors.

Cabling faults also hide. An intermittent run does not fail a meeting loudly; it causes the slightly unreliable connection, the camera that drops at night, the port everyone quietly avoids. Each incident gets blamed on the device, the provider or the Wi-Fi — while the cause sits in a ceiling void. Certified cabling removes that whole category of doubt: every link is measured, and the report says so.

What does “done right” actually include?

It starts on paper, not on a ladder. The cabling is designed from the floor plan: how many points per desk, where the access points and cameras sit, where the rack lives, and which routes the containment takes. Spare capacity is planned at this stage — extra points at desks, spare capacity in the rack — because adding a point during first fix costs a fraction of adding one after handover.

Then the materials and the standard. Cat6A is the safe default for new installs: it carries 10-gigabit speeds over the full channel distance and handles the higher PoE loads modern access points and cameras draw. Fibre forms the backbone between floors, buildings and data rooms, fusion-spliced and terminated by trained fibre technicians. Installation follows international structured cabling standards rather than ad-hoc wiring — the difference shows in the rack: patch panels dressed neatly, cable management that keeps it serviceable, and every point labelled at both ends — outlet, patch panel and schedule — with as-built documentation handed over at completion.

Why does certification matter more than the cable brand?

Because a reel of excellent cable installed badly is a bad network. Certification is what converts “we installed it” into “we measured it”: every copper link is Fluke-tested, every fibre run gets an OTDR trace, and the results are supplied per link. If a link fails, it is fixed and re-tested before sign-off — not discovered by your team in the first month.

The reports are also your long-term asset. Years later, when you add a floor, move a department or chase a fault, the certification records and labelling tell the next engineer exactly what is in the walls. Undocumented cabling is a debt every future IT project has to repay; certified, documented cabling is infrastructure you can build on.

What does bad cabling cost you later?

Mostly time and access. Fault-finding in finished ceilings means opening ceilings; adding points in an occupied office means working around people, often out of hours; replacing cabling that was never labelled means tracing runs by hand. Each job is small — together they are the standing tax of a building that was cabled cheaply.

There is also a ceiling on what you can adopt. The next access-point generation, higher-power cameras, a second screen at every desk — each assumes the cabling can carry it. Offices that cabled to yesterday’s minimum end up re-cabling to reach tomorrow’s baseline, in an occupied building, at retrofit prices.

When should cabling be planned in a fit-out or move?

At design stage, alongside the fit-out programme — cabling is first fix. The runs go in before ceilings close; the second fix — outlets, patching, testing — lands ready for move-in day, so points are live when the desks arrive rather than a week after.

Already occupied? Retrofit and re-cabling are still entirely possible: the inherited cabling is audited first — keep what certifies, replace what does not — and the work is phased around your operations, out of hours where needed. It simply costs more per point than doing it during the fit-out, which is the whole argument for doing it once, and doing it right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cat6 or Cat6A — which do we need?

Cat6A is the safer default for new installs: it carries 10-gigabit speeds over the full channel distance and handles higher PoE loads for Wi-Fi access points and cameras. We confirm the right choice for your site and budget at survey.

Do you certify the cabling you install?

Yes — every copper link is Fluke-tested and fibre runs are OTDR-tested, and you receive the certification reports for every point we install. Faults found in testing are fixed and re-tested before sign-off.

Can you cable an office that’s already occupied?

Yes — retrofit and re-cabling work is phased around your operations, with containment and routing planned to keep disruption low, and out-of-hours work where needed.

Do you install fibre between buildings?

Yes — we install, splice and OTDR-test fibre for building-to-building and campus links, and where fibre isn’t practical we install wireless point-to-point bridges instead.

What documentation do we get at handover?

Every point labelled at both ends — outlet, patch panel and schedule — plus the certification reports per link and as-built documentation of the installation, so any engineer can work on the system years later.
